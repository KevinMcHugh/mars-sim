# Tile art

> Part of the [mars-sim documentation](./README.md).

## What it is

Hand-drawn SVG that the browser map draws in place of a terrain's emoji. The
first (and so far only) piece is the built wall: a running bond of rust bricks
instead of 🧱, whose look varies wildly between emoji fonts and reads as a
pile of loose bricks when tiled.

## Source

- `web/src/map/tileart.ts` — `TILE_ART`, SVG keyed by the glyph it replaces;
  the brick wall is generated by `brickWall()`.
- `web/src/map/renderer.ts` — `setHello` finds each symbol with art and hands
  it to `buildAtlas` alongside the species-pack sprites.
- `web/src/map/atlas.ts` — paints each SVG over its glyph's atlas cell once it
  decodes (the same path as [species-pack](./species-pack.md) sprites).
- `web/src/map/tileart.test.mjs` — pins that the wall has art and that art is
  plain SVG.

## How it works

The atlas draws every symbol as emoji first, then paints any `AtlasImage`
over its cell and re-uploads the texture. Tile art is just more of those
images, matched by glyph string, so nothing on the wire or in the engine
changes: the Hello still names 🧱, the hover line, legend and TUI still show
it, and if the SVG fails to decode the map keeps the emoji.

The terrain shader samples a glyph across the whole tile (`fract(vTile)`), so
art drawn to the cell's edges joins its neighbours. The brick wall is built to
tile in both directions: 128 units square, four 32-unit courses (an even
number, so the offset courses line up top to bottom) of 64-unit bricks (two
per course, so the bond lines up side to side), with half the mortar gap on
each side of every seam.

## Why it is this way

- **Art in the frontend, not the wire.** Species-pack sprites travel in the
  Hello because they belong to a world's roster; a wall looks the same in
  every world, so it is a constant beside the renderer.
- **Edge-to-edge, not a centered icon.** A centered brick sticker leaves floor
  showing between wall tiles, and a room outline reads as a dotted line. Full
  bleed makes walls read as walls.
- **Split bricks share a tone.** In an offset course the brick at x = −32 is
  the far half of the brick at x = 96 on the tile to the left, so the two are
  given the same tone; otherwise every tile seam shows as a colour change.

## Extending it

Add an entry to `TILE_ART` keyed by the glyph from `internal/glyphs`. Keep the
`0 0 128 128` viewBox (the atlas cell), draw to the edges if the terrain should
join its neighbours, and keep the SVG self-contained: no scripts and no
external `href`s (it is decoded as an image, which would ignore them anyway).
